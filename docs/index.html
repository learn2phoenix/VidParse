Abstract

Translating continuous, noisy egocentric video into discrete, temporally ordered action steps is fraught with visual challenges. Heavy ego-motion, transient occlusions, and the high intra-class variability of unscripted human–object interactions cause frame-level online temporal models to struggle, often resulting in severe over-segmentation and structural collapse. We present VidParse, an online, training-free framework that treats activity understanding as a graph-constrained inference problem. Rather than relying on learned temporal filters, we identify semantic transitions using a temporal similarity matrix over manipulation-anchored features extracted from frozen foundation models, prioritising foreground hand–object interactions. A beam search decoder then leverages an induced procedural task graph to enforce valid action transitions and prune impossible trajectories. By anchoring robust visual segments to hard procedural constraints, our approach preserves long-range state transitions and achieves up to a 10× improvement in multi-step parsing accuracy over strong online baselines, without a single gradient update.

Method

Three pieces, none of them learned. The bet is that a frozen foundation model already sees enough, and what is missing is the right place to look and the right constraint on what can follow what.

Manipulation-anchored features

A frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 gives patch tokens; a frozen hand–object detector gives the interaction box. We average only the tokens inside it — training-free masked attention that follows the hands, not the kitchen behind them.

frame → (1024,)

Training-free boundaries

Actions appear as homogeneous blocks in a temporal similarity matrix, so a Gaussian-tapered checkerboard kernel slid down its diagonal turns block edges into novelty peaks. Nothing is fitted, and nothing is buffered beyond the kernel.

kernel 2 s · peak height 9 · min gap d = 20

Graph-constrained decoding

Segments are scored against k-means action prototypes, then decoded by a beam search that may only follow edges of a task graph induced from the training sequences. Forbidden transitions cost infinity and are pruned.

B = 10

The constraint the decoder obeys

Induced from training sequences, never from test video. Click a step to see what must happen before it and what it enables — precisely what the beam search may not violate.

Does the parse keep its shape?

Frame metrics reward local agreement; they say little about whether a predicted sequence still makes sense as a procedure. N-step transition accuracy asks how many n-step action transitions survive, swept over progressive completion of each video. The gap widens with n: at five and seven steps our AUC is several times the baseline's, which is the sense in which the structure survives and a frame-level model's does not.

Against a frame-level model

ProTAS predicts per frame and flickers near boundaries; segment-level decoding under a graph constraint does not. Background frames are removed for legibility.
